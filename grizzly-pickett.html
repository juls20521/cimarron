Grizzly Pickett

Teamster

Subject: Grizzly Pickett

Basic Information

  • Character Name: Pinkney Algernon Pickett
  • Nickname: Grizzly
  • Hometown: Beaufort, South Carolina
  • Occupation: Independent Freight Teamster & Salt Hauler (Covert Labor Exploiter)
  • Age: 41
  • Relationship Status: Widowed

Appearance

Tall and broad. He sports long, graying brown hair, a rugged beard, and weather-worn features. He wears heavy canvas work clothes, high leather boots, and a wide-brimmed slouch hat. Face claim: Trace Adkins

Personality

  • Imposing: He uses his size to naturally dominate any room or camp circle without him having to raise his voice.
  • Calculated & Cold: War and prison camps have burned out his empathy, leaving him to view people strictly as cargo, leverage, or utility.
  • Deceptively Decent: His way of learning who is alone is to be briefly, believably kind to them—a bought meal, a patient ear, the right gentle questions. The warmth is a tool; he is taking a man's measure even as he buys him a drink.
  • Ruthlessly Pragmatic: He will leave an injured man on the flat to die rather than spend the water hauling him back, or dangle false word of a captive's family to make him work harder. It's simple arithmetic.
  • Resentful: He harbors a bitter grudge against Northern corporate bosses lining their pockets while he lives in the dust.
  • Touchy About His Past: Beneath his gruff, mountain-man exterior lies a sharp insecurity regarding his soft birth name and fallen aristocratic roots.

Motivations & Disposition

Desires: Earn enough money through his illicit hauling contracts to eventually buy out his employer or purchase his own legitimate freight line.

Secrets: He transports captive forced-laborers to a hidden salt work in the Playas Valley on behalf of a his employer, actively snatching vulnerable drifters along his route.

Fears: Being put back into chains or a prison cage. Having his true birth name exposed.

Biography

1834: Born Pinkney Algernon Pickett in Beaufort, South Carolina, to an affluent rice-planting family, raised under the weight of high-society expectations that ill-fit his massive frame.

1858: Marries his childhood sweetheart, settling into what he expects will be a comfortable life managing his family's coastal estate.

1861: Enlists in the Confederate cavalry at the outbreak of the Civil War, eventually serving under General Jubal Early. He leaves his young bride behind on the family plantation.

1863: While deployed in Virginia, Pinkney receives word that his wife has died from disease. Months later, federal forces destroy his family's estate, leaving him with nothing to return home to.

1864-1865: Captured during a skirmish in the Shenandoah Valley, Pinkney spends the final year of the war suffering in a freezing Union prison camp, emerging penniless, widowed, and thoroughly hardened by the ordeal.

1866-1868: Abandoning his aristocratic name and Southern roots, he heads west as "Grizzly." He scrapes together enough coin doing heavy labor to purchase a sturdy freight wagon and his first ox team.

1869-1873: Grizzly establishes himself as an independent teamster in the Southwest, driving long-haul routes through harsh territory and earning a reputation as a cold, unflappable man who asks no questions.

1874: Grizzly catches the eye of a ruthless operator running a salt harvest. Seeking steady coin, Grizzly accepts a contract to haul bulk industrial salt and agreeably takes on the job of bringing "replacement labor". He's paired up with the owners illegimate son (Derrick "Dirch" Brixton) and the pair don't always see eye to eye but have settled into an uneasy working relationship.

1875: With the expansion of the CRC line west, Grizzly sets his sights on the camp's transient population of vulnerable drifters. Lonely laborers provides the perfect hunting ground for his secondary cargo.
